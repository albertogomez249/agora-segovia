WHAT WE FOUND

Fourteen months here, and still the same four bars.

We asked 312 students in Segovia what their week actually looks like. The answers from international students were the sharpest, and they were all the same shape: the city is right there, and it stays shut.

Where these come from. The 99.1% is not ours: it is from the PwC Socioeconomic Impact Report of 2025, commissioned by IE University itself, for the 2023-24 academic year. The other three are ours, and smaller: a questionnaire answered by 312 students on both Segovia campuses between September and November, plus 23 interviews and 42 businesses visited in person. It is field research by students, not a representative survey — read the percentages as the shape of a problem, not as a measurement.

“Fourteen months here. I still go to the same four bars, because those are the ones somebody once took me to.”

Second-year international student · interview 11, November

It is not that Segovia is closed. It is that nothing here is written for you. The discounts exist — 31 of the 42 businesses we visited already give students a better price — but they are informal, unwritten, and in Spanish, and they live in conversations you are not part of.

One of the four barriers our research found was the language, and it goes further than a menu. It is in the whole transaction: working out whether a price is a student price, and whether the place is any good once you get there.

WHAT YOU GET

Leisure in Segovia, at a price written for you.

Two things a student does — buy something, or go to something — and seven categories between them.

A map, not a search box

The app opens on Segovia with a price on every pin. You are standing in a street with ten minutes to spare: the first screen answers what is near you and from how much, without a single tap.

Buy for less

Restaurants and products — the set lunch, the ponche, the shops in the centre and the ones in Nueva Segovia nobody shows you. Percent off, student price, two for one, or a bundle.

Go out for less

Clubbing, football, theatre, cinema and sport you play together. Tickets bought in the app at the student price, with the code on your phone at the door.

Reviews by people who actually paid

Only a student who bought there can review a place, and a moderator reads it before anyone else does. A venue cannot remove a review it does not like. That is the one thing a scraped review site cannot copy.

Top picks are earned, never bought

A venue can pay to be promoted, and that promotion is labelled as such. It cannot pay its way into top picks: those come only from moderated student reviews — three of them at least, averaging 4.3.

What your own campus does

Where students at IE or UVa actually go, filtered down to your degree. Counts only, never names — and never a group under five people, because in a city this size a count that small points at individuals. The same floor applies to how many students used a listing: under five, we show no number at all.

Show a code, keep your card

Nothing to print and nothing to carry — the card lives in the app. For a discount you show six digits at the till and pay the venue as you always would. Only things you book ahead, like a ticket or a court, are paid in the app, and that money sits with us until the venue confirms your code. Do not turn up, and it comes back to you.

Written in both languages

Every venue profile and every offer exists in English and Spanish, because the business writes it once and our assistant does both.

THE OBVIOUS QUESTIONS

Two things people ask before they believe any of this.

“What do the businesses get?”

A table empty at four on a Tuesday earns nothing, and most of these venues already give students an informal discount they get no credit for. We bring a customer who would not otherwise have come, and we charge 8% of that sale, only when it happens — no listing fee, no monthly cost, nothing to lose by trying. The venue sets its own price and its own hours, and can stop at any time.

It is also the answer to a fair worry: Segovia has a complicated relationship with IE students. This sends them to places outside the four bars, in the quiet hours, at a price the business chose.

“How is this not Google Maps?”

Maps tells you a restaurant exists. It cannot tell you there is a student price, what it is, or whether anyone from your course thought it was worth it. Three things here do not exist anywhere else:

A verified price. You see it because your university email says who you are. That is why it is not on the open internet.

Reviews only from people who paid. Not anyone with an account — the app already knows who bought there, and a venue cannot delete what it does not like.

Written for someone who does not speak Spanish. Every profile, every offer, both languages, because the business writes it once and the assistant does the rest.

HOW IT WORKS

Three steps, about twenty seconds.

Verify with your university email

Your @student.ie.edu or @ie.edu address, a six-digit code, done. That verification is the whole point: it is what makes a student price a student price, and it is why these prices are not on the internet for everyone.

Find something tonight

Browse by what it is or by where you are. Each listing shows how many students have used it and whether it was checked this week, so you are never walking to an offer that died in March.

Pay in the app and show the code

The venue confirms your six digits at the till and the money is released. If you never turn up, nobody is charged. Your savings are counted on your card, so you can see exactly what the thing is worth to you.

STRAIGHT ANSWERS

What people asked us when they tried it.

Does it cost anything?

No, and it will not. There is no fee, no subscription and no premium tier for students. The businesses pay 8% of a sale when one happens, which is the whole model.

Do I have to put money in to use it?

No. A discount is a code you show at the till and you pay the venue as you always would. Only things you book in advance — a ticket, a court, a table held for you — are paid in the app, and that money sits with us until the venue confirms your code. If you never turn up it comes back to you, and anything unspent is yours to withdraw.

Is the discount taken off before or after I pay?

Before. The price you see in the app is the price you pay. For a percentage off, the figure shown is an example on a typical spend — the percentage comes off whatever you actually buy.

Who can see what I buy?

Nobody, individually. Your campus page shows counts and never names, and never a group smaller than five people, because in a city with two campuses a count that small describes individuals rather than a trend.

Why do you need my university email?

Because that is what makes a student price a student price. It is also why these prices are not on the open internet for everyone. We use it to verify you and to tell you when something you asked about is live — nothing else.

Can a business delete a bad review?

No. Only a student who actually paid there can write one, a moderator reads it before it appears, and the venue cannot remove it. A venue can pay to be promoted, and that promotion is labelled; it cannot buy its way into top picks.

What if the venue does not honour the price?

Tell us and you are refunded in full. It is also the fastest way for a venue to be removed: the whole thing only works if the price in the app is the price at the till.

Is any of this real yet?

The software is. The deals are not: every discount, price and review in the prototype was written by us, and none of the businesses named has agreed to anything. We are saying so here rather than letting you find out at a till.
